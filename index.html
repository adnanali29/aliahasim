Why It Matters

Mental Health & Well-being

Mental health is not merely the absence of illness — it is the presence of vitality, resilience, purpose, and the capacity to navigate life with self-awareness and grace. In a world that increasingly demands more from us — more productivity, more performance, more perfection — the quiet inner landscape of our psychological well-being is often the first thing we sacrifice.

Yet this sacrifice carries a profound cost. When mental health is neglected, it quietly erodes the quality of every relationship, every decision, and every experience we have. Anxiety tightens around possibilities. Depression dims the colours of ordinary joy. Unprocessed stress calcifies into physical illness. The mind and body are not separate systems — they are one, constantly in conversation with each other.

Understanding mental health means understanding the human condition itself. It means recognising that emotional pain is not weakness. It means acknowledging that seeking support is not failure — it is perhaps the most courageous act a person can take. It means creating a world where vulnerability is honoured, not hidden.

"Mental wellness is not a luxury for the privileged few — it is a fundamental human right, and it is the foundation upon which everything else in life is built."

For communities in India — where mental health carries the weight of stigma, cultural silence, and generational trauma — this conversation is especially urgent. We are a society that has long equated emotional expression with weakness. My work is dedicated to dismantling that narrative, one conversation, one session, one classroom at a time.

The Four Pillars of Well-being

1. Emotional Awareness

The ability to identify, understand, and express emotions without judgement — the bedrock of psychological resilience.

2. Cognitive Clarity

Breaking patterns of negative self-talk, cognitive distortions, and limiting beliefs that hold us back from our potential.

3. Relational Health

Building safe, boundaried, and nourishing relationships with others — and most importantly, with oneself.

4. Purposeful Living

Aligning one's daily life with values, goals, and a sense of meaning that sustains motivation and inner peace.

I am Aaliyah Meraj Hashmi — a psychology lecturer, mental health educator, and certified guidance counsellor with a deep-rooted belief that every human being deserves to understand their own mind. My journey into psychology was not simply an academic pursuit; it was, and continues to be, a calling — a desire to bridge the profound gap between what people feel inside and what the world allows them to express.

I hold a Master's degree in Psychology, which gave me the theoretical and scientific grounding to understand human behaviour in all its complexity — from developmental psychology and personality theories to clinical assessment and abnormal psychology. But it was the Certified Course in Guidance that transformed my practice from knowledge into craft, equipping me with the practical tools to truly walk alongside another person through their inner world.

During my postgraduate years, I was fortunate to engage deeply with academic research — presenting three papers at State-level conferences and one at an International conference. Each paper was not just an academic exercise; it was a chance to contribute meaningfully to the growing discourse on mental health, counselling practices, and psychological well-being in the Indian context. These experiences shaped my understanding that psychological knowledge must not remain locked in journals — it must reach classrooms, communities, and conversations.

My clinical internship at the SCB Medical College, Department of Mental Health, was a formative and humbling experience. Surrounded by individuals who were navigating the most vulnerable chapters of their lives, I learned that the most powerful intervention a psychologist can offer is sometimes simply the act of truly listening — without agenda, without judgment, with complete presence. That internship became the compass for everything I do today.

Today, I serve as a Psychology Lecturer and Mental Health Educator, committed to making psychological literacy part of everyday life — not just in hospitals and clinics, but in schools, workplaces, families, and communities.

Areas of Practice

My Expertise

My practice spans six interconnected domains of psychological support — each grounded in evidence-based methodology and delivered with compassion, cultural sensitivity, and a deep respect for individual experience.

01

Mental Health Counselling

Providing a grounded, empathetic space for individuals navigating anxiety, depression, stress, grief, trauma, and emotional dysregulation. Drawing on cognitive-behavioural, humanistic, and integrative approaches to support lasting psychological change. Every individual brings a unique story — and every session begins with deep listening.

02

Career Counselling

Helping students, young professionals, and career-changers navigate the complex terrain of occupational identity and purpose. Through psychometric assessments, values clarification, and structured guidance conversations, I support individuals in making career decisions that align with their strengths, interests, and long-term vision — not just societal expectations.

03

Student Counselling

Academic pressure, examination anxiety, identity formation, peer relationships, and the transition from adolescence to adulthood — these are not trivial concerns. They are the defining experiences of a young person's life. I work with students to build psychological resilience, develop self-regulation strategies, and foster a healthy relationship with learning, failure, and success.

04

Lifestyle Counselling

Our habits, routines, relationships, and daily rhythms are not separate from our mental health — they are its architecture. I work with individuals to examine and reshape the lifestyle patterns that contribute to emotional distress, offering practical, psychologically-informed guidance on sleep, boundaries, digital well-being, relationships, and daily self-care that is sustainable and deeply personal.

05

Stress Management

Chronic stress is one of the defining health challenges of the modern era — yet most people have never been taught how to manage it effectively. My stress management work goes beyond surface-level tips, addressing the cognitive patterns, physiological responses, and behavioural cycles that sustain stress, and equipping individuals with evidence-based tools to restore equilibrium and inner calm.

06

Corporate Well-being

Workplaces are psychological environments. The culture, communication patterns, leadership styles, and workload norms of an organisation directly shape the mental health of everyone within it. I partner with corporate teams and organisations to deliver well-being workshops, burnout prevention programmes, team resilience sessions, and mental health awareness training that transforms workplace culture from the inside out.

My Approach

Philosophy & Practice

My practice is rooted in the belief that every person holds within them an innate capacity for healing, growth, and self-understanding — and my role is simply to help them access it.

Every person who walks into a counselling space — whether it is a clinic, a classroom, a workshop, or a community hall — carries with them a lifetime of experience, shaped by relationships, culture, history, and the countless stories they have told themselves about who they are. My fundamental philosophy begins here: with deep, unconditional respect for the complexity of each individual's inner world.

An Integrative, Person-Centred Approach

I do not subscribe to a single therapeutic doctrine, because human beings are too rich and varied to be served by only one lens. My practice draws from the Cognitive-Behavioural tradition — examining how thoughts, feelings, and behaviours are interlinked and how changing one can transform the others. It is also deeply informed by Humanistic psychology, particularly Carl Rogers' person-centred approach, which places the therapeutic relationship itself at the heart of healing. I also incorporate psychoeducation — the belief that understanding the science of the mind is itself a therapeutic act, empowering individuals with knowledge about their own psychology.

Cultural Sensitivity in Indian Contexts

Practising psychology in India means practising at the intersection of rich cultural heritage and significant social stigma. The notion that mental health concerns are signs of weakness, that emotional struggles should be kept within the family, that seeking professional support is shameful — these are not merely individual beliefs but deeply embedded cultural scripts. My work is acutely sensitive to these contexts. I meet people where they are, not where a Western psychological model might expect them to be. This means integrating cultural understanding, family dynamics, religious frameworks, and social realities into every interaction.

Education as Prevention

As a Psychology Lecturer, I am deeply committed to the idea that education is the most powerful preventive mental health tool we have. When young people learn about emotional regulation, cognitive distortions, stress physiology, and the neuroscience of trauma — they gain a vocabulary for their inner lives. They learn that their experiences have names, that they are not alone, and that change is possible. The classroom, for me, is as much a site of healing as the counsellor's room.

The Role of Community

Mental health is not solely an individual matter — it is a collective one. The environments we inhabit, the relationships we maintain, and the communities that surround us are the living infrastructure of our psychological well-being. My community outreach work, such as the Mother's Day session with NGO Envoy, reflects my conviction that the most transformative mental health work often happens not in clinical settings but in the warm, shared spaces of community life — where people recognise themselves in each other's stories and realise they are not alone in their struggles.
